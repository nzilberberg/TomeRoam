`loss = scrollTop − min(scrollTop, max(0, maxScroll_active − (var(--safe-top) + 51px)))`

— subtracted directly from the clamp on the line above, so the two expressions cannot disagree. **Zero for a home scrolled anywhere but the last `safe+51`px of its range; at most `safe+51`px otherwise (≈51px with no notch inset, ≈98-110px on a notched iPhone); and the full `scrollTop` only when home's entire scroll range is smaller than `safe+51`px** (a short library, which is a real and reachable case). ### 4.2 The fix — an invariant, and the one implementation that satisfies it

**INVARIANT P (the requirement — RESTATED over THREE axes; basis: current behaviour to preserve + the V3 derivation + the 4th strike's executed third axis).** *Parking `#home` may change only two things: **where the box paints**, and **whether it receives input**. It must not change (i) `#home`'s scroll range, its scroll-container status, its content width, or its block-direction padding; nor (ii) **whether the browser's scroll-anchoring machinery operates on the box.*** Under P the browser has nothing to clamp AND nothing to stop adjusting, so the offset survives the park and the un-park with no code involved, and a content mutation landing mid-park is absorbed exactly as it would be on an active home.

**Why the third axis exists, and why it is not a generalisation of the first two.** The 4th strike executed, in a real Blink engine with controls green, that scroll-anchoring participation is a function of the scroll container's own `transform` and `overflow` state — **not** of its geometry: a non-none `transform` suppresses every anchoring adjustment, and `overflow: hidden` un-suppresses it. Geometry equality therefore does not imply anchoring equality, which is precisely the inference the previous revision made (§8 R-writer-enum's falsified sentences). The park *must* carry a non-none transform — the transform is what puts the box off-screen, so it is the one park effect that cannot be given up — so the park is permanently in the suppressing state on axis (ii) unless something un-suppresses it. That is the whole reason `overflow: hidden` is retained.

**The implementation (prescribed, because exactly one design satisfies P without touching the ACTIVE recipe — U11). ONE deletion.** In `#home.parked` (css:98-103):
- **DELETE `top: 0` — the only deletion.** This is the whole geometric defect: a pre-6i in-flow vestige from when parking an in-flow `#home` needed to lift it out of flow. Post-6i the active `#home` is already `position:fixed` with both insets, so `top: 0` no longer lifts anything — it only resizes the box. (Same era, same shape and same fix direction as M2's vestigial `46`: the 6i/decouple work moved the real views to the fixed-inset model and left two pre-6i constants behind, one in the ghost builder and one in the park rule.) **The 4th strike confirms this deletion by execution and no finding touches it.**
- ⭐ **KEEP `overflow: hidden` — REQUIRED, not merely permitted.** With `top: 0` gone, `top` and `bottom` both cascade from `#home` (or from `body.has-player #home`), so the parked box is stretched to the identical inset height, `clientHeight` and `scrollHeight` match the active box, and `maxScroll` matches — **axis (i) is closed by the `top: 0` deletion alone, independently of `overflow`.** `overflow`'s only role is axis (ii): it is what keeps a transformed parked box participating in scroll anchoring, so a mid-park content mutation moves the offset parked exactly as it would active. Retaining it costs nothing on axis (i) — scrollbars are width/height 0 for `#home` (css:772-775), so no axis's scrollbar gutter can change `clientHeight`, and the strike's own measurement confirms it: parked-pre `clientHeight` came in at active + 71px with `overflow: hidden` present, i.e. exactly the `top` difference and nothing more, so `overflow: hidden` contributes ZERO to the box geometry.
- **KEEP** `transform: translateX(-101vw)`, `pointer-events: none`, `z-index: 0`, `will-change: transform` — the park's actual effects, none of which touches the scroll range. `will-change` is retained verbatim in both rules (§2 STAYS).

**The honest limit on axis (ii): it holds by a MEASURED engine behaviour, not by construction.** Parked and active reach the same anchoring participation by *different* routes — the active box anchors because it has no transform; the parked box anchors because a transform plus `overflow: hidden` anchors. ### 7.2 ⛔ ENVIRONMENT FIDELITY — the note is INVERTED, and the prohibition is the point (folds Charpy V1/F4 and F5)

**The measured fact is unchanged and now cuts the other way.** jsdom (29.1.1) stores `scrollTop` writes verbatim and never clamps: a value of 500 survives the park unchanged and a write while parked sticks. Under the RETIRED restore design that made two cells unable to fail. Under this design it makes the opposite mistake available: **a cell asserting "the scroll survives the park" would pass in jsdom no matter what `#home.parked` declares — vacuously green, because jsdom never clamps in the first place.**

**PROHIBITION (blocking, for Curie and for any future session).** No CI cell may assert that `#home.scrollTop` is preserved across a park/un-park, and no clamp shim may be built to enable one. jsdom cannot model the clamp, and modelling it would mean asserting the fix against a hand-written model of the very browser behaviour the fix is device-owed to confirm. **The preservation is DEVICE-owed (§9 R-M1-cause).** What CI proves instead is the two things it can prove honestly: the RECIPE (M1PARKRANGE — a static read of the stylesheet, which cannot be vacuous) and the WRITE SET (M1WRITERSET, M1NOWRITE, M1NAVWINS — write observations, which do not depend on any clamp). **The clamp shim prescribed in the previous revision is RETIRED, not re-specified:** with no restore line there is no cell that needs it, so Charpy F4(a) (the asynchronous `MutationObserver`), F4(b) (the shim keyed to computed `overflow`, a non-cause — and the correct cause is now §4.1's cascade, which is a third reading, not F4(b)'s) and F4(c) (the unmodelled parked-write half, which existed only to support the retired design's "the write clamps to 0 → harmless" claim, a claim this design does not make) are all resolved by removal.

**SECOND PROHIBITION (blocking, added by the 4th strike) — no CI cell may assert SCROLL-ANCHORING behaviour, and none may be credited with INVARIANT P's third axis.** jsdom implements no scroll anchoring at all: it never adjusts an offset when content above it changes size, so a cell that mutated home's content while parked and asserted "the offset moved with the content" would fail for the environment's reason, and one that asserted "the offset did not move" would pass for the environment's reason. Both readings are fictions about the engine. **What CI proves is that the `overflow` declaration is PRESENT (M1PARKRANGE's Tier 0, a static read); what buys the behaviour is the engine, and the behaviour is observed on device (§9 R-M1-anchor) or on a real desktop Blink bench.** The same applies to the write-observation cells: an anchoring adjustment is not a scripted write, so M1NOWRITE and M1NAVWINS are neither strengthened nor weakened by it and must not claim the axis. **The lesson the 4th strike attached to this: "not executable in jsdom" is NOT the same as "device-owed."** A real desktop engine sits between the unit harness and the device, it is the engine of a shipped target (the Android WebView APK), and "the same in both states" claims are exactly what it settles cheaply. Route an engine-behaviour claim there before filing it as device-owed.

**The write recorder (Charpy F5, folded and PROMOTED to load-bearing).** M1NOWRITE and M1NAVWINS have no residual-value oracle to fall back on, so the recorder IS the oracle and its specification must be exact:
- **Mechanism.** `Object.defineProperty` on the `#home` ELEMENT with a get/set pair over a backing field, pushing each write into an array. `scrollTop` is a configurable accessor on `Element.prototype` (measured), and jsdom has **no** `Element.prototype.scrollTo`/`scrollIntoView`, so the harness's existing `window.scrollTo` recorder (app-harness.js:266) is a function-property replacement and is the WRONG precedent. The right precedent is in the repo: `test/browse-decouple.test.js:264-266` does exactly this on `#browse`, and `test/browse-render-race.test.js:26-30` repeats it.
- **Scoping — two named windows, stated once and identically everywhere.** The recorder is ARMED after the fixture's own setup write and read as two windows: **window A** = the external navigation step (M1NAVWINS only), **window B** = the clock advance that fires the `settleTimer` and runs `runFinalize`. The oracle is a COUNT and a VALUE per window: M1NOWRITE expects zero writes in B (it has no A). M1NAVWINS expects **exactly one write of value 0 in A** (nav.js:140's deliberate reset) and **zero writes in B**. This is deliberately not "no non-zero write" — that weakening collapses the oracle back into a value check and stops catching a zero-valued policy violation (a future writer touching `#home` harmlessly is still a second writer, which is the thing M1WRITERSET exists to forbid).
- **Reachability guard, per cell.** A no-write oracle is satisfied perfectly by a fixture that never arrives. M1NOWRITE guards reachability by also asserting `#home.scrollTop` still reads the seeded value (it would be 0 if the finalize had taken the `resetScroll:true` path) and by asserting the abort's reveal ran. M1NAVWINS guards it with the aborted gesture's own `ghostY`: `cover.ghostY` is set from `cur.ghostY` (app.js:1154) and printed in the reveal FLASH line as `ghostY=<n>` (app.js:1105), with precedent for asserting on it (`test/swipe-gesture.test.js:387-389`) — note that line is emitted by the reveal watcher's `finish()` on a 500ms timer (app.js:1116), so under `fakeTimers` the clock must be advanced past 500ms to read it, AFTER the window-B assertions. The synchronous alternative is the ghost clone's own `transform: translateY(-500px)` (swipe.js:292) while the pane is still mounted; Curie picks one per cell rather than inventing a third.
- **The third interval, named so it is not mistaken for window B.** Reading the `ghostY=<n>` guard requires advancing past 500ms AFTER the window-B assertions, which opens an interval that is neither A nor B. **No write is asserted in that third interval** — its only purpose is to emit the reveal FLASH line for the reachability guard. A future reader must not treat the guard advance as an extension of window B, and must not add an assertion to it without saying which window it belongs to.

### 7.3 Mutation registration — the tooling remedy, and THREE non-unique anchors: two already-live and one this plan registers (folds Charpy F3, F14, F18)

`tools/mutate.mjs` is an explicit anchor list of `{name, file?, from, to, also?}` literal substitutions with no generative operators. A `from` that does not occur prints `ANCHOR NOT FOUND` and exits 1 (mutate.mjs:741-743); `test/mutation-anchors.test.js` fails when an anchor rots; a no-op (`from === to`) is refused. **What nothing checks is UNIQUENESS:** the applier is `src.replace(from, part.to)` (mutate.mjs:745) — `String.prototype.replace` with a string pattern replaces the FIRST occurrence only — and `mutation-sweep.mjs` reports a failure COUNT (`caught (${failures} failing)`, mutation-sweep.mjs:211), never WHICH cell killed the mutant. A mutant whose anchor occurs at two sites therefore mutates the wrong one, dies to the wrong cell, and reports `caught` while the intended site is never mutated. **That is this campaign's own defect shape — a cell credited with a crossing it never drove — reproduced inside the tooling built to detect it.**

**BUILD WORK (Brunel), gated by MUTUNIQ (§7.1):**
1. **A non-unique `from` is a HARD ERROR.** Count occurrences before applying; refuse unless the entry disambiguates, and say the count in the message. Two admissible disambiguations: a longer `from` carrying enough surrounding context to be unique (already this table's convention — see the PTR anchors, mutate.mjs:620-624), or an explicit occurrence selector on the entry (`count: 1` asserting uniqueness, or `occurrence: N` selecting one of N — the entry declares which, so the tool never guesses). Apply the same check to `also` parts, resolved against `part.file || m.file || DEFAULT_FILE`.
2. **`test/mutation-anchors.test.js` fails on a registered non-unique anchor**, not merely on a rotted one — so the defect is caught at registration rather than at sweep time.
3. **The sweep names the killing cell.** Print the failing test/cell identity beside `caught`, so a sweep result can be read against the expected-killing-cell each entry in §7.1 now declares.

**FIVE NON-UNIQUE ANCHORS — non-uniqueness is the DEFAULT hazard here, not an exception.** Five of five anchors inspected in this campaign are non-unique: two already-live registrations (`#24`, `#42`) and three this plan is about to register (M1NOWRITE's `resetScroll: false`, and both of M1PARKRANGE's mutants — see the CSS bullet below). **State it plainly, because it changes how every registration in this plan must be written:** an anchor in this repo is to be assumed non-unique until the tool proves otherwise, so a `from` carrying enough surrounding context to be unique is the **DEFAULT form for a new entry, not a fallback** for the cases someone happens to notice. Two consequences follow. **(1)** §10's sequencing of the tooling remedy FIRST is load-bearing rather than tidy — at a 3-of-3 rate, an unhardened applier makes every mutation result in this repo unreadable, including this plan's own. **(2)** The hazard is not a property of these three entries that re-anchoring retires; it is a property of a literal-substring applier over a 3,200-line file, and only the mechanical uniqueness check retires it. Re-anchoring the three without landing the check would leave the next entry exposed.

**Re-anchor all three and re-verify what each proves:**
- **`#24` `'swipe: abort stops restoring the starting scroll (-> I7)'` (mutate.mjs:270-271).** Its anchor `window.scrollTo(0, cur.scroll0);` occurs **three times** in `js/app.js`: **app.js:445 (the SUPERSESSION RECOVERY)**, app.js:1203 (the HELD abort path), app.js:1228 (the NO-HOLD abort path).
