## 2. Scope and feasibility verdict

**What this filing DECIDES (from source):**
- The reframed approach — keep active `#home` `position:fixed`/own-scroll — WOULD remove the tall→short document reflow that repositions the carousel scroll-layers (§4). This is the mechanism the camera pins the flash to, and a fixed element does not reposition on a document collapse. Feasibility of the MECHANISM is confirmed from source; it is continuous with 6g, not a fresh violation of the no-promote invariant.
- The approach is NOT free and NOT a single-decision change: active `#home` genuinely USES the shared document scroll today, consumed by pull-to-refresh (app.js:1340,1347), the custom scrollbar (scrollbar.js:52 `doc:true` branch), and navbar-seating (`body.home-tall`, nav.js:81). Converting `#home` to a self-contained fixed own-scroll container re-homes each of these onto `#home`'s internal scroll and overturns the ratified §2.1 invariant.

**What this filing does NOT commit:** any code change. The build is BLOCKED on the D1–D3 derivation (§5), because committing it would either overturn §2.1 on partial greps or rest on an unverified "home does not need document scroll" assumption — both forbidden by U1/EC §2.

**Boundary of the eventual change (stated so the derivation is scoped, not so it is pre-approved):**
- MOVES to `#home`'s own scroll model: the vertical scroll of home content; the "at top of home" signal pull-to-refresh reads; the scrollbar's home tracking; navbar seating for home.
- STAYS: the outgoing app-ghost machinery; the frozen swipe model + spec + oracle (§8); 6f's in-flow→overlay outgoing app-ghost; `holdGhostUntilPaintable` for `abort→browse`.
- SPLIT across the seam: `→home` construction (incoming: `home-snapshot` → the real fixed `#home`; `browse→home` outgoing: `real-source` → app-ghost, closing 6f's owed browse→home outgoing item).
- DEFERRED: the `→browse`/`→overlay` extension (§9); the device confirmation of the flash (§6 R1, the standing oracle).

## 3. The reframed approach — fixed own-scroll `#home`, slide-and-leave

The proposal, in one line: **active `#home` never leaves `position:fixed`.** Parked `#home` is already `position:fixed; transform: translateX(-101vw)` (css:103-107). The reframe keeps it fixed when ACTIVE too — the transition into home slides the real fixed `#home` from its off-screen `translateX` to `translateX(0)` and LEAVES it there as the active view. `#home` is its own scroll box (an `overflow-y` scroller with its own height), like the overlay class's scroll box (§2.1 line 145) — not an in-flow document-scrolled view.

Consequences that make it a NO-SWAP reveal:
- **No home-snapshot.** The reason the snapshot exists (let home be present alongside the in-flow `#browse` when two in-flow views can't coexist, §2.1) DISSOLVES: a fixed `#home` is not an in-flow view, so it can coexist with the in-flow `#browse` directly — exactly as parked `#home` already coexists with active `#browse` today.
- **No cover-drop / uncover.** The incoming mover IS the real `#home`, on-screen and rastering throughout the slide — never occluded behind a cover. There is no moment where an occluded real carousel layer is uncovered all at once (the frame the camera caught).
- **No tall→short document reflow of `#home`.** `#browse` leaving flow at commit still collapses the DOCUMENT height, but a fixed `#home` is positioned against the VIEWPORT, so its carousels do not reposition when the document collapses (the reposition Linnaeus §5 identifies as the underived re-raster trigger). The document scroll clamp is on the document, which the fixed `#home` ignores.
- **Outgoing** (`browse→home`): the real `#browse` becomes an app-ghost (`ghostApp`, already the recipe for in-flow→overlay/browse→browse outgoing) so the real `#browse` is never a transformed mover — CLOSING the browse→home outgoing item 6f left owed (subsystem §23). `overlay→home` outgoing stays `real-source` (an overlay is additive/own-scroll and does not hold the document height).
- **Leave it:** at finalize the mover's inline transform is cleared (app.js:775) → the fixed `#home` rests at `translateX(0)`; the outgoing ghost drops off-screen (`dropPanes`) invisibly; `applyScreen(home,{render:false})` reconciles visibility with NO hold.

This is the "slide the real screen in and leave it" the assignment asks for, made viable by removing the constraint that forced the swap (§2.1) rather than ignoring it — the constraint is removed by moving `#home` out of the in-flow scroll class, continuous with 6g's promotion exception.

## 4. Feasibility from source — does it remove the reflow, and what it costs

**Does it remove the reflow the camera implicates? YES (source-derivable mechanism).** Linnaeus PROBE-home-carousel §5 pins the flash to the carousel scroll-boxes being REPOSITIONED to a new viewport y as the tall `#browse` document collapses to short `#home` under the cover, then rastered-from-empty at the uncover. Two source facts make a fixed `#home` avoid this: (1) a `position:fixed` element is laid out against the viewport, not the document, so a document-height collapse does not move it; (2) with no snapshot cover, `#home`'s carousels are on-screen for the whole slide, so they are never occluded-then-uncovered. The document still collapses when `#browse` leaves flow, but nothing the user is looking at (the fixed `#home`) moves with it. The mechanism is sound from source.

**What it costs — the document-scroll consumer surface (derived here; completeness is D2).** Active `#home` today is an IN-FLOW view sharing the document/window scroll (§2.1). At least three consumers read that assumption and would regress if `#home` became a fixed own-scroll container without being re-homed:
- **Pull-to-refresh** (app.js:1332-1358). Arms only when home is the current screen AND `!$('home').classList.contains('parked')` AND `window.scrollY > 0` is FALSE (i.e., document at top) — app.js:1340, and re-checks `window.scrollY > 0` on move (app.js:1347). If `#home` becomes fixed/own-scroll, `window.scrollY` is always ~0 (the document is empty/short) while the real home position lives in `#home.scrollTop`; pull-to-refresh would then arm even when home is scrolled down — a regression. It must read `#home.scrollTop` instead.
- **The custom scrollbar** (scrollbar.js:52). For a document view it returns `{ top: window.scrollY, total: se.scrollHeight, view: innerHeight, doc: true }`. Home's scrollbar tracks the DOCUMENT scroll. A fixed own-scroll `#home` needs its scrollbar re-homed to `#home` (`doc:false`, `top: #home.scrollTop`, `total: #home.scrollHeight`).
- **Navbar seating** (`body.home-tall`, nav.js:81; css:73/81). `home-tall` gives the DOCUMENT real height "to seat the fixed navbar" while home is active. A fixed `#home` makes the document short regardless; navbar seating must not depend on the document height for home. `body.home-tall` and the `scrollTo(0,1)` home-entry write (nav.js:127) become moot for home and are replaced by `#home`'s own height + `#home.scrollTop = 0`.

The existence of pull-to-refresh, a custom scrollbar, and `home-tall` is itself EVIDENCE that home is expected to be a SCROLLABLE view (they would be pointless if home always fit the viewport) — so a fixed `#home` needs a real `overflow-y` scroll mechanism with a defined height, not `overflow:hidden`. That is a re-architecture of home's scroll model, not a one-line construction flip — which is exactly why the derivation (§5) must precede the build.

## 6. Risk registry

- **R1 — Does the transform-clear/un-park re-raster the carousels EVEN with a fixed `#home` and no reflow?** (Device-only, load-bearing.) Removing the parked `translateX(-101vw)` to `translateX(0)` is still a compositor event on a layer whose descendants are the two `-webkit-overflow-scrolling` carousels (Linnaeus swap-necessity §5, home-carousel §7). Hypothesis: with the carousels never repositioned (fixed `#home`) and on-screen for the whole slide, there is no raster-from-empty — but this is exactly the underived compositor fact, settleable ONLY on the proven scrolled `browse→home` 60fps repro (scroll down → flash today / clean from top). **Device step (the now-proven oracle):** capture a scrolled `browse→home` commit at 60fps and confirm no one-frame carousel blank at the slide's end. **Fallbacks if it still flashes:** (F1) keep `#home` layer-promoted so there is never a demote (already in force, css:118 — reused, not re-added); (F2) slide via an ANCESTOR/wrapper transform so `#home`'s own layer (and its carousel sublayers) is never transform-mutated; (F3) a non-transform slide (`left`/`translate` on a wrapper). Do NOT claim the flash fixed without the device step.
- **R2 — Home scroll-model conversion regresses a document-scroll consumer** (pull-to-refresh app.js:1340/1347, scrollbar scrollbar.js:52, navbar-seating nav.js:81, and any D2 finds not yet enumerated). Mitigation: the build's scrub re-homes each named consumer to `#home.scrollTop`; D2 makes the set complete. A missed consumer is a silent home regression — this is the primary reason to derive before building.
- **R3 — Overturning the ratified §2.1 invariant (two in-flow / shared document scroll).** Moving `#home` out of the in-flow scroll class is NEW POLICY (EC §4.19), not parity. Mitigation: reconcile §2.1/§2.4 in the plan-of-record, the subsystem contract, and the frozen model, and ledger the policy — as an explicit, dated overturn, never a silent edit.
- **R4 — Aborted-swipe snap-back on the real view.** An aborted `browse→home` must restore `#browse` and re-park `#home`. If `#home` stayed fixed throughout the drag, the abort re-parks it (slide back to `translateX(-101vw)`) and re-shows `#browse` — potentially simpler than today's snapshot-retreat, but it must not re-decode `#browse` covers on the return (the `.178/.179` abort-flash class). Mitigation: the abort restores `#browse` via the existing `abort→browse` reveal (`holdGhostUntilPaintable` WITHOUT the 6h scroll-settle gate — preserved) OR keeps `#browse` painted-off-flow; D2/the build decide. Assert both sides (I7: after abort, document/home scroll equals gesture-start — subsystem §12, plan-of-record I7).
- **R5 — `overlay→home` band exposure.** `overlay→home` rides the same SNAPSHOT rule; the outgoing overlay stays `real-source`. If the base under the overlay was a tall `#browse`, un-parking a fixed `#home` behind an inset overlay (options z25) could expose the collapse through the topbar/navbar bands (the 6f device-verified precondition). Mitigation: same enumerated-opaque-overlay precondition as 6f (subsystem §23); confirm on device; `browse→home` (full-cover outgoing ghost) is the primary target and is unaffected.
