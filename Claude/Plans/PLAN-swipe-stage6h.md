## 3. The load-bearing promise and the single fracture for Loki

**The load-bearing promise (single, SCOPED to the commit→home held reveal).** *On a commit→home reveal (`opts.scrollSettle` set), the cover pane is removed EXACTLY ONCE, and never before a scroll-settle signal — a real `window` `scrollend`, or the bounded settle backstop, or the 600ms safety-net — has had its chance to fire after the under-cover `scrollTo(0,1)` collapse; and the cover is ALWAYS eventually removed (it can never strand), with every session-owned reveal handle (the double-`rAF` frame, the 600ms net, the `scrollend` listener, the settle-timeout) retired at that one removal. The abort→browse reveal is byte-unchanged.*

Concretely: the gate predicate is `decoded && painted && settled`; on a commit→home reveal WITH a large outgoing clamp (`cur.scroll0 > SETTLE_SCROLL_MIN`) `settled` starts `false` and is flipped only by `scrollend`/settle-timeout; on every other path — including a small-clamp / top commit→home (`cur.scroll0 ≤ SETTLE_SCROLL_MIN`) and abort→browse — `settled` starts `true`. `drop()` is `dropped`-guarded, so at most one removal occurs; the 600ms `cur.revealTimer` calls `drop('timeout')` unconditionally (not through `gate`), so at least one removal always occurs even if `settled` never flips. The two new handles are retired in the same `drop()` that retires the existing two. **The conditional does not weaken the lifecycle promise (Loki-verified, 1022 interleavings):** it only ROUTES the small-clamp case to the already-verified non-`scrollSettle` fast path — the same path abort→browse and every pre-6h reveal already take. No new interleaving is introduced by the routing; the settle-gate interleavings are exactly those of the large-clamp case Loki struck.

**The single fracture Loki attacks — the cover is STRANDED, DOUBLE-DROPPED, or the listener ACCUMULATES UNBOUNDED.** A stranded cover (the view stays hidden behind the home snapshot forever) is a WORSE bug than the flash; a double drop (two `fadePanes`/two `sessionDone(cur)`) corrupts session ownership. The promise fails iff some interleaving of {decode resolve, double-`rAF`, `scrollend`, settle-timeout, 600ms-net, a superseding gesture} either (a) reaches no `drop()` at all — e.g. if a mutation routed the 600ms net through `gate()` so it now respects `painted && settled`, and the view never paints → strand (this is the true worse-than-flash guard, cell STRAND); or (b) reaches `drop()` twice — e.g. if the `dropped` guard were removed, `scrollend` and the settle-timeout both fire and both drop; or (c) **leaks the `scrollend` listener** — `drop()` fails to `removeEventListener`, so the listener stays on `window` after the session ends and ACCUMULATES one more per commit→home gesture without bound. **⚠️ Correction (Charpy B2): a leaked listener is INERT, not a spurious-drop hazard** — a later `scrollend` reaching it calls `gate`/`drop` on the finished session, which is a `dropped`-guarded / `cur !== session` no-op. So the real cost of a leak is UNBOUNDED `window`-listener accumulation across a session's lifetime (a memory/CPU creep), not a wrong drop. Loki's strike: drive a commit→home held reveal and construct the interleaving that strands the cover (a via the 600ms-net path with `painted` false), drops it twice, or leaves the `scrollend` listener attached after `drop()` (observable as a growing `window` listener set across repeated gestures).

**Handed to Loki explicitly (so a blind strike does not false-clear):** the 600ms `cur.revealTimer` is the INTENTIONAL never-strand backstop — it calls `drop('timeout')` DIRECTLY, bypassing the `settled` gate, precisely so an unsupported/never-firing `scrollend` (or a never-painting view) cannot strand the cover. A strike that suppresses `scrollend`/settle-timeout and finds the cover STILL removed at 600ms has found the working backstop, not a fracture. The fracture requires DEFEATING that backstop (or duplicating the drop, or leaking the listener). Also handed over, so it is not misread as a leak: **under supersession there is a BOUNDED, TRANSIENT double-listener** — the superseded session's `scrollend` listener remains on `window` until that session's own `drop()` fires (at latest its 600ms net), while the successor's listener is already attached; this is at most one extra listener per concurrently-pending reveal (a small constant, retired at each drop), NOT the unbounded accumulation of fracture (c). The new handles inherit the EXISTING stale-continuation treatment of `revealFrames`/`revealTimer` (retired only in `drop()`), so a superseded session's own 600ms net still fires `drop()` and retires everything. A strike on the transient-double-listener path has found the accounted-for parity, not a new fracture.

**⚠️ THE DEVICE-ONLY HONESTY FINDING (read this before believing the fix).** The promise above is about the GATE MECHANISM — that the cover waits for a real scroll-settle signal and can never strand — and that is fully CI-provable (§8). It is NOT a promise that the flash is gone, and it rests on `scrollend` behaving as hoped. There are TWO distinct device-only risks, named separately (Charpy H1):

- **Risk 1 — `scrollend` EXISTENCE (does the primary signal fire at all?).** A programmatic INSTANT `scrollTo(0,1)` may emit NO `scrollend` on iOS — the event is best-specified for user and smooth scrolls, and an instant jump can complete without one. If it does not fire, the primary signal never arrives and the gate degrades SILENTLY to the `SETTLE_MS` heuristic timeout. In that world the "PRINCIPLED" claim is hollow: the fix is effectively a tuned hold. **This is the load-bearing unknown for the classification** — see §4.
- **Risk 2 — `scrollend` SUFFICIENCY (does firing on it drop late enough?).** Even if `scrollend` fires, it signals scroll-POSITION completion, while the flash is the compositor RE-TILE of newly-exposed content during the snap — `scrollend` may fire BEFORE the re-tile finishes, so dropping on it is still too early and the flash survives.

Both are invisible to CI: iOS compositing/rasterisation is off the main thread and invisible to jsdom/rAF (the saga's withdrawn frame-detector lesson). The ONLY oracle is the user's scroll-down repro (scroll down→flash / top→clean). **To make Risk 1 OBSERVABLE, the device-verify is bound to the drop `via=` / settle-source log** (§9): the shipped `drop()` already logs `via=${why}`, and this slice stamps which signal flipped `settled` — so a device repro shows `via=scrollend`/`settle=scrollend` (the principled path fired), `via=settle`/`settle=settle` (the heuristic backstop released it — `scrollend` never came, Risk 1 realized), or `via=timeout` (even the settle-timeout was gated out — the never-paints strand path). This turns "did `scrollend` even fire" into a read off the log, not a guess. **This stage is NOT "confirmed fixed" until the repro is clean on device AND the log shows the intended `via`.** If Risk 2 bites (clean `via=scrollend` but still flashing), the remaining lever is a heuristic hold of N extra composited frames AFTER `scrollend` — deferred tuning (§10), honestly a heuristic, not a principled gate.

## 4. The settle-signal decision — `scrollend` primary + bounded backstop

The gate needs a signal that the iOS COMPOSITOR has finished the under-cover scroll-collapse snap. The double-`rAF` "paint" gate cannot provide it (main-thread only). Two candidate signals were assessed; the design uses the first as primary and the second as a bounded backstop.

**Candidate A — `window` `scrollend` event (CHOSEN, primary — principled).** `scrollend` fires when a scroll operation completes, including a programmatic `scrollTo`, and on iOS the document scroll is compositor-driven — so `scrollend` rides the scroll/compositor timeline the double-`rAF` is blind to. It is the exact signal both Linnaeus probes recommend. It is groundable and instrumentable (a test dispatches a synthetic `scrollend`; on device the drop-`via` reason is logged). **Why principled, not a tuned delay:** it is a real event tied to the actual scroll completing, not a guessed duration. **Grounded caveats, stated honestly:** (1) iOS Safari support began 17.4 (uneven on older devices) — the backstop below covers absence; (2) it is dispatched asynchronously after the synchronous `applyScreen` returns, so a listener attached inside `holdGhostUntilPaintable` (after the scroll write already issued at nav.js:127) still catches it — the compositor snap is precisely what has NOT finished when the main thread returns; (3) SUFFICIENCY (does the re-tile finish by `scrollend`?) is device-only (§3).

**Candidate B — a scrollY-stability poll across rAFs (REJECTED as the signal).** Rejected on the exact ground the assignment names: `applyScreen` already issued `scrollTo(0,1)` synchronously, so `window.scrollY` reads `1` (settled) on the main thread IMMEDIATELY (`preDrop=1/900` in both flash and no-flash cases). A scrollY-stability poll would read "settled" instantly and miss the compositor snap entirely — it would be a no-op dressed as a gate. Not used.

**The scroll-magnitude threshold `SETTLE_SCROLL_MIN` (CHOSEN — the conditional-engagement fix).** The settle gate engages only when `cur.scroll0 > SETTLE_SCROLL_MIN` (§2). Read from `cur.scroll0` (app.js:466 — the outgoing scroll at gesture start; the |Δscroll| that clamps). A viewport-relative default is preferred over a magic px so it scales across devices: `SETTLE_SCROLL_MIN ≈ 0.5 · window.innerHeight` (roughly half a screen). **The calibration obligation (honest):** the threshold must be LOW enough that the smallest outgoing scroll that actually flashes still engages the gate, and HIGH enough that a top / near-top reveal (the no-flash case) stays on the fast path. The device repro (scroll down→flash / top→clean) calibrates it; the hard invariant is only that `cur.scroll0 ≈ 0` MUST take the fast path (both the regression fix and the clean-anyway case). Bias toward engaging (do not ship a flash) while clearly exempting the top case.

**The bounded backstop (heuristic, NON-primary) — MINIMAL, because the fallback lands on the COMMON path.** Because `scrollend` may be unsupported (old iOS), degenerate (a `scrollTo` that moves nothing emits no `scrollend`), or simply not fire for an instant programmatic scroll (§3 Risk 1 — LIKELY), a session-owned settle-timeout (`SETTLE_MS`) also flips `settled`. **Critical (user correction): SCROLLED-DOWN is the COMMON case** (users scroll the list, then swipe back to Home), so the gate engages on the common path — and if `scrollend` does not fire there, `SETTLE_MS` becomes the COMMON-case hold duration. A conservative ~250ms would therefore delay the common back-to-Home reveal by ~250ms — a real perceptible regression. **So `SETTLE_MS` MUST be the MINIMAL duration that still covers the compositor snap: short enough to be imperceptible (a few frames), long enough to hide the snap.** It is a device-tuned BAND, not a fixed value: a FLOOR (`SETTLE_MS ≥ the compositor snap duration`, or it fires before the snap completes and still flashes) and a CEILING (small enough to be imperceptible on the common path). Grounded default: **~80–120ms** (a few frames past the snap; my grounded call), explicitly TUNABLE via the device `via=`/`settle=` log (§3): `via=scrollend` → the short principled path fired (good); `via=settle` → the backstop fired, so tune `SETTLE_MS` DOWN toward the observed snap duration until the hold is imperceptible but still covers the snap. Do NOT default it to 250ms.

**Invariant: snap-floor `≤ SETTLE_MS < 600` (the existing reveal net).** Two bounds: (a) `SETTLE_MS ≥ the snap duration` (the floor above — below it the backstop fires mid-snap and still flashes); (b) `SETTLE_MS < 600` so it fires BEFORE the never-strand net (else the settle-timeout is dominated by the net and adds nothing). Keep `SETTLE_MS` distinct from 340/600 so it is identifiable in `pendingDump` (§9).

**The settle-source stamp (for the device-observable classification).** When `settled` flips, the flipping signal is recorded (`cover.settleVia = 'scrollend'` or `'settle'`) and appended to the existing FLASH log line, so the device repro shows WHICH path released the cover (§3 binding). This is a diagnostic stamp only — no behavior depends on it.

**Overall classification: PRINCIPLED IF `scrollend` fires on a programmatic scroll; else a bounded heuristic hold — and the device `via`/`settle` log tells us which (Charpy H1).** The design is principled by intent — the primary release is a real timeline event (`scrollend`) — but whether that intent holds is exactly §3 Risk 1, and it is only settleable on device. So the honest classification is conditional, not asserted: if the device log shows `via=scrollend`, the gate is principled; if it shows `via=settle`, the fix is effectively a tuned `SETTLE_MS` hold. Either way the cover is never stranded and never worse than today. No feature-detection branch is used (it would add a code path the harness cannot exercise without property-shimming, and "always attach + backstop" is simpler and strictly covers the no-`scrollend` case).

## 5. Lifecycle / ownership section (the two new session-owned handles)

The managed resources are the `window` `scrollend` listener and the settle-timeout, both created only on the commit→home (`scrollSettle`) path.

- **CREATE / acquire.** Both are created inside `holdGhostUntilPaintable` immediately after the paint/decode gates are wired, only when `opts.scrollSettle` is set. The listener is created via `window.addEventListener('scrollend', onSettle)`; the timeout via `setTimeout(..., SETTLE_MS)`. Owner: the current session object `cur` (the same owner that already holds `cur.revealFrames` / `cur.revealTimer`).
- **BORROW.** The `window` object is BORROWED, not owned — the slice adds a listener to it and MUST remove exactly that listener; it never replaces or disposes `window`. This is the borrowed-vs-owned distinction (EC §4.4): the listener registration is the owned resource layered on a borrowed host, so cleanup removes the registration and leaves the host intact.
- **HOLD / MUTATE across the async wait.** Between creation and `drop()`, the two handles are held on `cur`. The only state they mutate is the local `settled` flag (set `true` on fire) and then a `gate()` re-check — no external mutation. They coexist with the still-pending double-`rAF` and 600ms net on the same owner.
- **RELEASE.** At `drop()` (the single owner endpoint for the held path — EC §4.5), the listener is released via `cur.revealScrollEnd()` (which calls `window.removeEventListener('scrollend', onSettle)`) and the timeout via `clearTimeout(cur.revealSettleTimer)`, in the same block that already cancels the double-`rAF` and the 600ms net. Release is exactly-once because `drop()` is `dropped`-guarded.
- **DISPOSE / destroy.** There is no separate disposal beyond release; the handles are not transferable and do not outlive the session. **The `drop()` removal is what keeps the `window` `scrollend` listener set BOUNDED** — every commit→home gesture adds one listener and its `drop()` removes exactly that one (cell OWN spies this removal directly). Omitting the removal is not a wrong-drop hazard (a leaked listener is inert — §3), but it accumulates listeners on `window` without bound across a session's lifetime. On supersession (a new gesture before this reveal drops), the superseded session's 600ms net still fires `drop('timeout')`, which disposes both new handles; between supersession and that drop there is a BOUNDED transient double-listener (§3, §6), not a leak. This is the SAME single-endpoint discipline the existing `revealFrames`/`revealTimer` already follow (retired only in `drop()`), deliberately reused rather than inventing a parallel supersession-side retirement (EC §4.21 narrow scope).
- **FAILURE.** If `scrollend` never fires and the settle-timeout is somehow cleared without flipping `settled`, the 600ms net still calls `drop()` directly and disposes both handles — the cover is removed and the resources released. A failure to release is impossible without also defeating the 600ms net (the §3 fracture). No error path throws: `removeEventListener`/`clearTimeout` on unset handles are no-ops.

## 6. Async / concurrency section (the race, ordering, supersession, never-strand)

**The new asynchronous surface.** The commit→home cover-drop, previously gated on two async signals (decode microtask + double-`rAF`) plus one direct-drop timer (600ms net), gains two more async inputs: a `scrollend` event and a settle-timeout. The drop now composes FIVE async producers over one `dropped`-guarded consumer.

**Temporal ordering (the guarantee).** `drop()` may run only after `decoded && painted && settled` (via `gate()`), OR unconditionally when the 600ms net fires. On the commit→home path `settled` is initially `false`, so the EARLIEST a gated drop can occur is after a `scrollend` or the settle-timeout — i.e., the cover provably outlives the bare double-`rAF`, which is the entire point (assert both sides: cover PRESENT after paint-but-before-settle; cover REMOVED after settle — §8 GATE, EC §4.7). On every other path `settled` is `true` from the start, so ordering is identical to today.

**The race, resolved.** Among {`scrollend`, settle-timeout(`SETTLE_MS`), 600ms-net, decode, double-`rAF`}, whichever combination first satisfies the predicate (or the 600ms net firing) wins and calls `drop()`. `drop()`'s `dropped` guard makes it exactly-once — every later producer that fires reaches a `dropped`-guarded no-op. The losers are cancelled inside that same `drop()` (the two existing cancels plus the two new retirements), so no parallel producer survives to touch a finished session (EC §4.3/§4.6).

**Supersession / cancel / abort.** A superseding gesture (begin()'s narrowed gate) can arrive while the reveal is pending. The reveal is session-scoped: `drop()` calls `sessionDone(cur)` (which no-ops when `cur !== session`), and the two new handles are `cur`-owned, so the superseded session's own 600ms net disposes them; a `scrollend` that arrives after the superseded session's `drop()` reaches a removed listener (nothing) or a `dropped`-guarded no-op. During the window between supersession and the superseded session's `drop()`, TWO `scrollend` listeners coexist on `window` (old + new) — this is BOUNDED (at most one per concurrently-pending reveal, a small constant) and each is retired at its own `drop()`; it is NOT the unbounded accumulation of a leaked-removal (§3 fracture (c)), and Loki is handed it explicitly so it is not misread as one. The new handles inherit the EXISTING stale-continuation treatment of `revealFrames`/`revealTimer` (Stage 6b/6c) rather than inventing a parallel one — the narrowest change consistent with EC §4.21.

**Failure / error / reject / never-strand (load-bearing).** The `scrollend` listener and settle-timeout can BOTH fail to release the cover (unsupported event; a `scrollTo` that moves nothing; a hidden tab that never composites). The never-strand guarantee does NOT rest on either: the pre-existing 600ms `cur.revealTimer` calls `drop('timeout')` directly, bypassing the `settled` gate, so the cover is removed within 600ms in every interleaving. No promise rejects and no listener throws; the design adds no new rejection path.

**Concurrency note.** Single-threaded event loop; no shared-memory parallelism. "Concurrent" here means multiple pending async producers over one consumer, resolved by the exactly-once guard above.
