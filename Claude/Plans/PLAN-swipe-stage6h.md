## 3. The load-bearing promise and the single fracture for Loki

**The load-bearing promise (single, SCOPED to the commit→home held reveal).** *On a commit→home reveal (`opts.scrollSettle` set), the cover pane is removed EXACTLY ONCE, and never before a scroll-settle signal — a real `window` `scrollend`, or the bounded settle backstop, or the 600ms safety-net — has had its chance to fire after the under-cover `scrollTo(0,1)` collapse; and the cover is ALWAYS eventually removed (it can never strand), with every session-owned reveal handle (the double-`rAF` frame, the 600ms net, the `scrollend` listener, the settle-timeout) retired at that one removal. The abort→browse reveal is byte-unchanged.*

Concretely: the gate predicate is `decoded && painted && settled`; on the commit→home path `settled` starts `false` and is flipped only by `scrollend`/settle-timeout; on every other path `settled` starts `true`. `drop()` is `dropped`-guarded, so at most one removal occurs; the 600ms `cur.revealTimer` calls `drop('timeout')` unconditionally (not through `gate`), so at least one removal always occurs even if `settled` never flips. The two new handles are retired in the same `drop()` that retires the existing two.

**The single fracture Loki attacks — the cover is STRANDED or DOUBLE-DROPPED.** A stranded cover (the view stays hidden behind the home snapshot forever) is a WORSE bug than the flash; a double drop (two `fadePanes`/two `sessionDone(cur)`) corrupts session ownership. The promise fails iff some interleaving of {decode resolve, double-`rAF`, `scrollend`, settle-timeout, 600ms-net, a superseding gesture} either (a) reaches no `drop()` at all — e.g. if a mutation routed the 600ms net through `gate()` so it now respects `settled`, and `settled` never flips → strand; or (b) reaches `drop()` twice — e.g. if the `dropped` guard were removed, `scrollend` and the settle-timeout both fire and both drop; or (c) leaks a handle onto a superseded gesture — e.g. the `scrollend` listener not removed at drop, so a later scroll on a NEW gesture's reveal flips a dead session's flag. Loki's strike: drive a commit→home held reveal and construct the interleaving that strands the cover, drops it twice, or lets a retired listener/timer act on a successor.

**Handed to Loki explicitly (so a blind strike does not false-clear):** the 600ms `cur.revealTimer` is the INTENTIONAL never-strand backstop — it calls `drop('timeout')` DIRECTLY, bypassing the `settled` gate, precisely so an unsupported/never-firing `scrollend` cannot strand the cover. A strike that suppresses `scrollend` and the settle-timeout and finds the cover STILL removed at 600ms has found the working backstop, not a fracture. The fracture requires DEFEATING that backstop (or duplicating the drop, or leaking a handle). Also handed over: the new handles inherit the EXISTING stale-continuation treatment of `revealFrames`/`revealTimer` (retired only in `drop()`, not in a separate supersession path) — a superseded session's own 600ms net still fires `drop()` and retires everything, and a `scrollend` reaching a superseded session's `drop()` is idempotent (`dropped`-guarded; `sessionDone(cur)` no-ops when `cur !== session`). A strike on that path has found the accounted-for parity, not a new fracture.

**⚠️ THE DEVICE-ONLY HONESTY FINDING (read this before believing the fix).** The promise above is about the GATE MECHANISM — that the cover waits for a real scroll-settle signal and can never strand — and that is fully CI-provable (§8). It is NOT a promise that the flash is gone. Whether dropping ON `scrollend` actually eliminates flash A is **DEVICE-ONLY**, for two grounded reasons: (1) iOS compositing/rasterisation is off the main thread and invisible to jsdom/rAF (the saga's withdrawn frame-detector lesson) — no harness can observe the flash; (2) `scrollend` signals scroll-POSITION completion, and the flash is the compositor RE-TILE of newly-exposed content during the snap — it is plausible `scrollend` fires BEFORE the re-tile finishes, in which case dropping on it is still too early and the flash survives. The ONLY oracle is the user's scroll-down repro (scroll down→flash / top→clean). **This stage is NOT "confirmed fixed" until that repro is clean on device.** If `scrollend` proves insufficient on device, the remaining lever is a heuristic hold of N extra composited frames AFTER `scrollend` — named as deferred tuning (§10), not built here, and honestly a heuristic, not a principled gate.

## 4. The settle-signal decision — `scrollend` primary + bounded backstop

The gate needs a signal that the iOS COMPOSITOR has finished the under-cover scroll-collapse snap. The double-`rAF` "paint" gate cannot provide it (main-thread only). Two candidate signals were assessed; the design uses the first as primary and the second as a bounded backstop.

**Candidate A — `window` `scrollend` event (CHOSEN, primary — principled).** `scrollend` fires when a scroll operation completes, including a programmatic `scrollTo`, and on iOS the document scroll is compositor-driven — so `scrollend` rides the scroll/compositor timeline the double-`rAF` is blind to. It is the exact signal both Linnaeus probes recommend. It is groundable and instrumentable (a test dispatches a synthetic `scrollend`; on device the drop-`via` reason is logged). **Why principled, not a tuned delay:** it is a real event tied to the actual scroll completing, not a guessed duration. **Grounded caveats, stated honestly:** (1) iOS Safari support began 17.4 (uneven on older devices) — the backstop below covers absence; (2) it is dispatched asynchronously after the synchronous `applyScreen` returns, so a listener attached inside `holdGhostUntilPaintable` (after the scroll write already issued at nav.js:127) still catches it — the compositor snap is precisely what has NOT finished when the main thread returns; (3) SUFFICIENCY (does the re-tile finish by `scrollend`?) is device-only (§3).

**Candidate B — a scrollY-stability poll across rAFs (REJECTED as the signal).** Rejected on the exact ground the assignment names: `applyScreen` already issued `scrollTo(0,1)` synchronously, so `window.scrollY` reads `1` (settled) on the main thread IMMEDIATELY (`preDrop=1/900` in both flash and no-flash cases). A scrollY-stability poll would read "settled" instantly and miss the compositor snap entirely — it would be a no-op dressed as a gate. Not used.

**The bounded backstop (heuristic, NON-primary).** Because `scrollend` may be unsupported (old iOS) or degenerate (a `scrollTo` that moves nothing emits no `scrollend`), a session-owned settle-timeout (`SETTLE_MS`, ~250ms; exact value device-tunable) also flips `settled`. This bounds the extra hold when no `scrollend` arrives, instead of every commit→home reveal waiting the full 600ms net on such devices. **Honesty:** this backstop IS a tuned duration — a heuristic. It is NOT the mechanism claimed to fix the flash; it is a never-sluggish floor under the principled primary. On the flash-affected platform (iOS 17.4+), `scrollend` is the releaser and the backstop rarely bites.

**Overall classification: PRINCIPLED.** The primary release on the platform that flashes is a real timeline event (`scrollend`); the heuristic (`SETTLE_MS`) is only a bounded backstop for absence. The one honest asterisk (efficacy is device-only, and a heuristic frame-hold may be needed if `scrollend` fires too early) is on the face of the plan (§3), not dressed up. No feature-detection branch is used (it would add a code path the harness cannot exercise without property-shimming, and "always attach + backstop" is simpler and strictly covers the unsupported case) — so old browsers get at most a bounded `SETTLE_MS` hold, never a stranded cover, never worse than today's flash.

## 5. Lifecycle / ownership section (the two new session-owned handles)

The managed resources are the `window` `scrollend` listener and the settle-timeout, both created only on the commit→home (`scrollSettle`) path.

- **CREATE / acquire.** Both are created inside `holdGhostUntilPaintable` immediately after the paint/decode gates are wired, only when `opts.scrollSettle` is set. The listener is created via `window.addEventListener('scrollend', onSettle)`; the timeout via `setTimeout(..., SETTLE_MS)`. Owner: the current session object `cur` (the same owner that already holds `cur.revealFrames` / `cur.revealTimer`).
- **BORROW.** The `window` object is BORROWED, not owned — the slice adds a listener to it and MUST remove exactly that listener; it never replaces or disposes `window`. This is the borrowed-vs-owned distinction (EC §4.4): the listener registration is the owned resource layered on a borrowed host, so cleanup removes the registration and leaves the host intact.
- **HOLD / MUTATE across the async wait.** Between creation and `drop()`, the two handles are held on `cur`. The only state they mutate is the local `settled` flag (set `true` on fire) and then a `gate()` re-check — no external mutation. They coexist with the still-pending double-`rAF` and 600ms net on the same owner.
- **RELEASE.** At `drop()` (the single owner endpoint for the held path — EC §4.5), the listener is released via `cur.revealScrollEnd()` (which calls `window.removeEventListener('scrollend', onSettle)`) and the timeout via `clearTimeout(cur.revealSettleTimer)`, in the same block that already cancels the double-`rAF` and the 600ms net. Release is exactly-once because `drop()` is `dropped`-guarded.
- **DISPOSE / destroy.** There is no separate disposal beyond release; the handles are not transferable and do not outlive the session. On supersession (a new gesture before this reveal drops), the superseded session's 600ms net still fires `drop('timeout')`, which disposes both new handles — so a superseded reveal cannot leak a live `scrollend` listener onto the successor's reveal. This is the SAME single-endpoint discipline the existing `revealFrames`/`revealTimer` already follow (retired only in `drop()`), deliberately reused rather than inventing a parallel supersession-side retirement (EC §4.21 narrow scope).
- **FAILURE.** If `scrollend` never fires and the settle-timeout is somehow cleared without flipping `settled`, the 600ms net still calls `drop()` directly and disposes both handles — the cover is removed and the resources released. A failure to release is impossible without also defeating the 600ms net (the §3 fracture). No error path throws: `removeEventListener`/`clearTimeout` on unset handles are no-ops.

## 6. Async / concurrency section (the race, ordering, supersession, never-strand)

**The new asynchronous surface.** The commit→home cover-drop, previously gated on two async signals (decode microtask + double-`rAF`) plus one direct-drop timer (600ms net), gains two more async inputs: a `scrollend` event and a settle-timeout. The drop now composes FIVE async producers over one `dropped`-guarded consumer.

**Temporal ordering (the guarantee).** `drop()` may run only after `decoded && painted && settled` (via `gate()`), OR unconditionally when the 600ms net fires. On the commit→home path `settled` is initially `false`, so the EARLIEST a gated drop can occur is after a `scrollend` or the settle-timeout — i.e., the cover provably outlives the bare double-`rAF`, which is the entire point (assert both sides: cover PRESENT after paint-but-before-settle; cover REMOVED after settle — §8 GATE, EC §4.7). On every other path `settled` is `true` from the start, so ordering is identical to today.

**The race, resolved.** Among {`scrollend`, settle-timeout(`SETTLE_MS`), 600ms-net, decode, double-`rAF`}, whichever combination first satisfies the predicate (or the 600ms net firing) wins and calls `drop()`. `drop()`'s `dropped` guard makes it exactly-once — every later producer that fires reaches a `dropped`-guarded no-op. The losers are cancelled inside that same `drop()` (the two existing cancels plus the two new retirements), so no parallel producer survives to touch a finished session (EC §4.3/§4.6).

**Supersession / cancel / abort.** A superseding gesture (begin()'s narrowed gate) can arrive while the reveal is pending. The reveal is session-scoped: `drop()` calls `sessionDone(cur)` (which no-ops when `cur !== session`), and the two new handles are `cur`-owned, so the superseded session's own 600ms net disposes them; a `scrollend` that arrives after the superseded session's `drop()` reaches a removed listener (nothing) or a `dropped`-guarded no-op. The new handles inherit the EXISTING stale-continuation treatment of `revealFrames`/`revealTimer` (Stage 6b/6c) rather than inventing a parallel one — the narrowest change consistent with EC §4.21.

**Failure / error / reject / never-strand (load-bearing).** The `scrollend` listener and settle-timeout can BOTH fail to release the cover (unsupported event; a `scrollTo` that moves nothing; a hidden tab that never composites). The never-strand guarantee does NOT rest on either: the pre-existing 600ms `cur.revealTimer` calls `drop('timeout')` directly, bypassing the `settled` gate, so the cover is removed within 600ms in every interleaving. No promise rejects and no listener throws; the design adds no new rejection path.

**Concurrency note.** Single-threaded event loop; no shared-memory parallelism. "Concurrent" here means multiple pending async producers over one consumer, resolved by the exactly-once guard above.
